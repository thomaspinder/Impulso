# Student-t observation errors use the scale-matrix convention

Impulso gains a second seam on the observation side: an `ErrorDistribution` process, sitting alongside the volatility process, that owns *which law the observation error follows* while the volatility process keeps owning *how big it is*. Two adapters ship: `Gaussian` (the default, today's behaviour) and `StudentT`, which registers `pm.MvStudentT` on the same manual Cholesky factor. Under the t, `Ω = L Lᵀ` is the **scale** matrix, not the covariance: `E[y_t] = μ_t` for `nu > 1` and `Cov[y_t] = nu/(nu−2)·Ω` for `nu > 2`. `FittedVAR.sigma()` keeps returning Ω unchanged, identification keeps factorising Ω, and the covariance is available from a new accessor, `FittedVAR.innovation_covariance()`.

The degrees of freedom are always exposed in the posterior as a deterministic named `nu`, whether fixed (`StudentT(nu=5.0)`) or inferred (`StudentT()`, the string shorthand's default). Inference puts a *shifted* Gamma prior on the excess degrees of freedom — `nu_excess ~ Gamma(2, 0.1)`, `nu = 2 + nu_excess` — rather than truncating a prior at 2. `Gamma(alpha=2, ·)` has zero density at the origin, so the prior vanishes exactly where `nu/(nu−2)` diverges, and the unconstrained NUTS transform is the ordinary positive-support one. The lower bound of 2 is hardcoded, not configurable: below it the t has infinite variance and every variance-shaped consumer in the library stops being defined.

## Considered options

- **Covariance convention — have `sigma()` return `nu/(nu−2)·Ω` under the t.** Rejected on two counts. It violates the layering established in ADR-0001: the volatility process owns Σ and hands out its Cholesky factor, so making the *error distribution* rewrite that output puts one seam's parameter inside another seam's return value. And it costs a round trip on the forecast path — the covariance would have to be deflated back to a scale factor before every innovation draw, since the scale mixture is defined against Ω, not against the covariance.
- **A separate `nu` field directly on `VAR`.** Rejected: it would be meaningless under `error_dist="gaussian"`, and the adapter-object pattern (`volatility=`) already handles per-law configuration without polluting the top-level spec.
- **Sampling the mixing variables explicitly as latents.** Rejected: `pm.MvStudentT` marginalises them analytically, which is both faster and better-mixing than a Gibbs-style augmentation. Per-observation weights would be a nice diagnostic and can be reconstructed post hoc from `(nu + n)/(nu + q_t)` if wanted.

## Consequences

- **FEVD is exactly invariant.** Shares are built from `Θ = Φ P`; rescaling `P → cP` multiplies numerator and denominator by `c²`, which cancels. No correction is needed or wanted, and the method's docstring says so, so nobody "fixes" it later.
- **Historical decomposition and counterfactuals are exactly invariant.** Both back out `ε = P⁻¹u` and re-propagate `Pε`. Under `P → cP` the shocks become `ε/c` and the propagated impact `Pε` is unchanged. Locked in as a test: the two run under Student-t errors and return numbers bit-identical to the Gaussian sibling on a matched posterior.
- **Impulse responses carry the one genuine caveat.** A "unit shock" is one column of `P`, which factorises the scale matrix — one *scale* unit, equal to `sqrt((nu−2)/nu)` unconditional standard deviations (about 0.82 sd at `nu = 6`). Shapes, ratios and variance shares are untouched; only the level convention shifts. Multiply by `sqrt(nu/(nu−2))` to restore the one-standard-deviation convention.
- **Conditional forecasting and structural scenarios are deferred, not silently approximated.** The Waggoner–Zha constrained draw and the ADPRR three-way partition are Gaussian conditional-law results, and the plausibility statistic's `χ²_r` reference assumes Gaussian shocks. Under a t the conditional law has updated degrees of freedom and a Mahalanobis-inflated scale, and the plausibility reference becomes an F/Hotelling statistic. Both methods raise `NotImplementedError` under a heavy-tailed adapter — a half-valid scenario is worse than a clear error, because nothing in the output would look wrong.
- **Stochastic volatility × Student-t is rejected at construction.** The degrees of freedom and the log-volatility innovation variance both absorb outliers, so they are only weakly identified jointly and NUTS mixes poorly. `VAR(volatility="sv", error_dist="student_t")` raises immediately rather than at fit time. Chan (2020) shows the combination is estimable with a purpose-built sampler; adopting it is a separate piece of work.
- **`ConjugateVAR` cannot host the t at all.** The Normal-Inverse-Wishart posterior is conjugate *to a Gaussian likelihood*; a scale mixture breaks conjugacy and would need a per-observation latent scale plus a Gibbs step. Heavy tails are a PyMC/NUTS-path feature.
- **Model comparison works out of the box on the PyMC backend.** `NUTSSampler` already passes `idata_kwargs={"log_likelihood": True}`, so `az.compare` between a Gaussian and a Student-t fit needs no extra plumbing. The nutpie backend ignores `idata_kwargs` (a pre-existing quirk, not introduced here), so comparison runs need `NUTSSampler(nuts_sampler="pymc")`.
- **Seeded Gaussian forecasts stay bit-identical.** The seam's RNG contract requires `rng.standard_normal` to be consumed first, and the Gaussian adapter consumes nothing else, so its stream is a strict prefix of the t adapter's. Regression pins on a seeded density forecast were captured before the seam landed and still pass.
